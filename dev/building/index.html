Building and editing

All of these are undoable steps in the window, and functions that return a new Crystal in code (see Working from Julia code).

New structure

File → New structure… starts from an empty cell (a b c, α β γ; then add atoms with Add) or a prototype filled with your elements and lattice constants: simple, body-centred and face-centred cubic, diamond, zincblende, rocksalt, CsCl, fluorite, perovskite ABX₃, hcp, wurtzite. Cubic prototypes take a, hexagonal ones a and c. The new structure opens in a new tab and is "Untitled" until saved. In code: prototype_crystal.

Editing atoms

The panel's Edit selected atoms row replaces the selected atoms by another element, deletes them (also the Delete key), moves them by a Cartesian vector in Å, or adds an atom at a fractional position. Atoms… opens the Edit atoms tool: for one selected atom, change its element and set its exact position (fractional or Cartesian); for several, change all their elements at once. Its Replace a species everywhere section turns every atom of one element into another (e.g. all As → P).

Changing elements keeps the bonds. After As → C in GaAs the Ga–C distance is still 2.45 Å, longer than the usual Ga–C limit, so the new pair inherits the old pair's limit and the status line says so (ViewSettings.bond_cutoffs).

In code: substitute, delete_atoms, move_atoms, add_atom, set_position, replace_species.

Supercells

Build → Supercell… makes repeats n1 n2 n3, or any cell whose vectors a′ b′ c′ are integer combinations of a, b, c (the columns of the supercell matrix). The Type menu has presets: √2×√2 R45° (square ab), √3×√3 R30° and rectangular 1×√3 (hexagonal ab). A preview shows the multiplicity, atom count and new cell. Primitive and Conventional (panel or Build menu) use spglib; Original goes back to the structure as opened. In code: supercell, primitive_cell, conventional_cell.

Shift, rotate and wrap

Shift atoms moves all atoms, or the selected ones, by a vector in fractional or Cartesian coordinates. Selected atom → origin fills in the shift that puts the selected atom at (0, 0, 0). Atoms that end up outside the cell are drawn where they are, not folded back in.

Rotate atoms turns all atoms, or the selected ones, by an angle about an axis through a centre; the cell does not turn.

  • Axis: a Cartesian direction, a lattice direction [u v w], the line from the first to the second selected atom (this also fixes the centre), or the viewing direction.
  • Centre: the centroid of the rotated atoms, the first selected atom, or a point.
  • Positive angles turn counterclockwise when the axis points toward you. Selected atoms turn as the copies you clicked, so a group that crosses a cell face turns as one piece.
  • The preview names the closest contact afterwards and flags it below 0.6 × the sum of covalent radii.

Wrap atoms into cell moves atoms outside the cell back in by whole lattice vectors, so fractional coordinates end up in [0, 1); the periodic structure does not change. Shift and Rotate have a switch to wrap afterwards. ViewSettings(wrap_atoms=true) draws every atom inside the cell regardless.

In code: translate_atoms, rotate_atoms, rotation_matrix, closest_contact, wrap, atoms_outside_cell.

Surfaces, slabs and superlattices

The Build surface tool (Build → Surface, superlattice…) cleaves a slab of the current structure along (h k l) with a number of layers, vacuum and termination (auto cuts where the fewest bonds break), or builds an A/B superlattice of the current structure with a second structure file, with B optionally strained in plane to A.

Superlattices are stacked from bulk blocks: interface spacings come from the blocks and strained layers get no Poisson relaxation, so relax them.

In code: slab, surface_cell, add_vacuum, superlattice, stack_cells.

Bridging atoms

Build → Oxygen bridge… (or Add oxygen bridge in the right-click menu) puts an atom into a bond. Select the two atoms of the bond, then a third atom (Shift+click, in that order).

  • The bridging atom (O by default; any element can be typed) goes to the middle of the bond plus a vector v of length offset (default 0.5 Å). v is perpendicular to the bond, in the plane of the three atoms, and points towards the third atom, so the new atom is inside their triangle.
  • Other side of the bond (−v) places it at −v instead; Flip last bridge moves a bridge you just added to the other side.
  • The preview shows the two new bond lengths, the bridge angle, the closest other atom, and the largest offset that stays inside the triangle.

The bond is not stretched, so relax the result. With Si and the default offset, Si–O is 1.28 Å and Si–O–Si 134°. In code: bridge_position, add_bridge.
